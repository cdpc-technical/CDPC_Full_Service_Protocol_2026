German Roaches
Eradicate German Roach infestations from customer homes using a multi-method approach including vacuuming, baiting, dusting, and IGR.
YEAR ROUND
Personnel Scope
Certified Pest Control Technician
Frequency
Initial Cleanout + Multiple Follow-Ups
Materials
Liquid insecticides, Sprayers, Bait Gun, Glue Boards, IGRs, HEPA Vacuum, PPE
PPE Required
GlovesSafety GlassesHatPantsBootsLong Sleeves
1
Prior to Arrival
Customers should be instructed to empty all kitchen cabinets and drawers prior to your arrival. Also to disinfect counters and shelving. Clean out behind appliances should also be recommended.
2
Arrival and Intro
Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory). Ask where they have been seeing roach activity and how long it has been an issue. Explain that their cooperation in following recommendations is vital to the success of the program.
3
Inspection

German Roaches will typically infest the kitchen prior to branching out. For small or new infestations, you may not see live roaches. Inspect every cabinet, drawer, shelve, etc.

Signs to look for: Excrement appears as tiny black dots around cabinet hinges, outlet faceplates, and windowsills. Molting and egg sacs may be on shelving and in drawers.

Large infestations will have a distinct odor. You may see roaches walking on countertops, walls, or dishes in the sink evidence of a lengthy infestation. No need to inquire as to why.

4
Control

Eradication requires a combination of the following methods:

🧹 Vacuuming

Great tool for immediately reducing numbers, reducing competition with baits, and less breeding. Vacuum up all you see include moltings and egg sacs (food sources for nymphs).

HEPA Vacuum
💨 Flush Out & Contact Kill

Spray Stryker 54 aerosol into cracks and crevices to flush roaches out for vacuuming. Can also be sprayed directly to roaches for contact kill.

Stryker 54 (Pyrethrin, Piperonyl Butoxide) CSI
🎯 Baiting Applications

CDPC uses two forms: Gel baiting and dry bait injection (Doxem Precise). Gel baits applied in small BB-sized balls to clean surfaces cabinet hinges, along shelf lines, inside outlets, etc. Use "bait tacos" (cut plastic from glue board or small cardboard squares) as a clean pallet. Doxem injected into crack and crevice, outlets, appliances, and hard-to-reach areas.

🌫️ Dusting

Explain the following to your customer:

1. Wait at least 30 minutes prior to placing things back in cabinets to allow product to dry.
2. Keep the area clean and spill free no competition with baits. Clean up excrement if found.
3. Don't move or throw away glue traps.
4. Don't use DIY sprays.
5. The office will contact them to set a follow-up for two weeks out. Explain that elimination of German Roaches will take more than one treatment especially for larger infestations.

4. Don't use DIY sprays can make treatment ineffective and push ants to other areas.
5. The office will contact them: 3 days for interior activity, 5 days for exterior.

1
Prior to Arrival Customer Checklist

Customer must be made aware:

• Must be out of the home for 90 minutes following treatment.
• Pets must be on a veterinarian-approved flea treatment.
• Pet bedding washed in hot soapy water and dried on high heat.
• Vacuum on the day of service prior to arrival.
• Pick up clutter from floors.

2
Arrival and Intro
Park in the road if possible. Clock in to FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory). Ask where they've been seeing fleas. Confirm flea checklist is complete.
3
Inspection
Inspect the entire home for flea activity. As you inspect, consider which areas are furthest from the exit that's where you'll begin treatment.
4
Control
🖌️ The Painting Method: Imagine painting the carpets and fabric surfaces with a light, even coat. Never walk back over areas you've painted, and always move toward your exit. This ensures even coverage, no missed zones, and no footprints through treated surfaces.
Using Alpine Flea & Bed Bug

1. Hold can upright, typically 3 feet above the surface (waist high).
2. Sweep side-to-side with 50% overlapping passes.
3. Maintain steady walking pace.
4. Ensure a light, even coverage not saturation.

Treat: Carpets, Rugs, Upholstered Furniture, Pet Sleeping Areas, under beds (if accessible).

Alpine Flea & Bed Bug (Dinotefuran, Pyriproxyfen, Prallethrin) BASF
Hardwood Floors

For rooms with hardwood floors: treat area rugs and upholstered furniture with Alpine Flea. Use B&G (Suspend Polyzone) on baseboards, and D-Fense NXT aerosol in crack and crevice where debris and pet hair gathers. Avoid application directly to hardwood floors creates a slick, slip hazard.

Suspend Polyzone (Deltamethrin) ENVUD-Fense NXT (Novaluron, Deltamethrin, Pyriproxyfen) CSI
⚠️ Cautions: Aquariums and fish bowls should be covered. Bird cages removed from treatment areas. Avoid open flames and lit pilot lights.
5
Communication & Follow-Up

• Allow 2 weeks for complete elimination.
• Continue heavy vacuuming daily for the next 14 days essential for hatching eggs.
• Flea activity may increase the first 7–10 days this is normal.
• We will follow up with them in 14 days.

Wasp Control
Eliminate active wasp nests, prevent new nest-building on the structure, and protect customers from stings. Wasps are a warrantied pest.
YEAR ROUND
Personnel Scope
Certified Pest Control Technician
Frequency
As Needed
Materials
Liquid & Dry insecticides, PPE, Sprayers, Granulator
1
Arrival and Intro
Park in road if possible. Clock in to FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory). Ask where they've been seeing wasps and how long. Reiterate that these are warrantied don't hesitate to contact us about stinging insects. Inquire about anything else they'd like addressed.
2
Inspection & ID

Inspect the entire exterior. Search for active nests under eaves, soffits, gutters, behind shutters, light fixtures, and patio furniture. Look for activity around structural entry points like roofline gaps and chimney caps.

Properly identify the pest:

1. Paper Wasp: Umbrella-shaped open comb, usually under eaves.
2. Yellow Jacket: Enclosed nests in voids or underground.
3. Hornet: Large football-shaped paper nests, often in trees or upper corners of structures.

3
Safety

1. Full PPE (gloves, uniform, eye protection).
2. Bee suit for heavy activity in tight areas.
3. Ensure pets/people are inside during knockdown.
4. Determine direction of escape path before approaching nest.
5. Use slow, controlled movements when positioning equipment.

⚠️ Note: Take extra precaution on hot midday treatments increases agitation.
4
Control
Exterior

Approach nests slowly. Use power-sprayer whenever possible saturate the nest and spot treat surrounding the nest sight to prevent reinfestation. Use Stryker Wasp & Hornet aerosol only when power-sprayer cannot be used (e.g., light fixtures on porch or patio).

With heavy activity, wait 10–15 min for mortality. Knock down nest with de-webber pole and dispose don't leave nests on the ground.

For entry points high on the structure: power-sprayer but don't flood. For entry voids out of reach: apply dust with de-webber head.

Route/Start Mixes (Any Season)Stryker Wasp & Hornet (Prallethrin) CSID-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) CSI
Interior

Use D-Fense Dust to treat voids, entry points, and fireplaces. For fireplace inserts, get dust behind the insert as best possible.

🚫 DO NOT use aerosols like Stryker around lit pilot lights.
5
Communication & Follow-Up
Follow up in 3 days for interior activity and 7 days for exterior activity. Exterior treatments and maintenance like those performed during regular quarterly services should prevent future nesting. Wasps are a warrantied pest don't hesitate to call if the problem persists.

2
Interior Inspection

Carpenter Ants found indoors are most commonly foraging workers from a satellite colony established inside the structure or workers from an outdoor parent colony entering to forage. Thorough inspection is critical before treatment.

High-priority areas: Under sinks and around plumbing in kitchens and bathrooms, window and door frames, areas around skylights, attic spaces, crawlspaces, and any area with a history of moisture damage or wood rot.

Frass identification: Carpenter Ant frass is coarse and fibrous - similar in texture to pencil shavings - and may contain insect parts. This distinguishes it from termite frass which is finer and pellet-shaped. Frass indicates an active gallery nearby.

Sound detection: In quiet conditions, tap on suspected wall voids. A hollow sound combined with rustling when disturbed can indicate an active Carpenter Ant gallery inside the wall.

3
Crack and Crevice Dust Application

Temprid Dust is the primary interior tool for reaching Carpenter Ants inside wall voids, structural voids, and hard-to-reach areas. Apply using a hand duster with a crack and crevice tip.

Application Areas

Apply Temprid Dust into wall voids via outlet plates, switch plates, and pipe penetrations. Treat behind and under kitchen and bathroom cabinets, inside door and window frame voids, along the sill plate in the basement or crawlspace, and any expansion joint or crack in the foundation accessible from the interior.

Void Drilling

If a gallery is suspected inside a wall void and no accessible entry point exists, a small drill hole (approximately 1/8 inch) may be made to allow dust injection. Plug the hole with a small piece of steel wool or a wall plug after application. Always communicate with the customer before drilling and document in FieldRoutes.

Application Rate

Apply a light, even coating of dust - do not over-apply. Heavy dust deposits are less effective than a thin residual layer and can alert ants to avoid the area. A light puff every 12-18 inches along a void is sufficient.

Temprid Dust (Beta-Cyfluthrin, Imidacloprid) - Envu
4
Gel Bait Placement

Apply Maxforce Fleet Ant Gel in small BB-sized placements along identified interior foraging trails and in areas where ant activity has been observed. Gel bait is a critical complement to dust and liquid treatments, providing a slow-kill food source that workers carry back to the colony - reaching ants that may not contact treated surfaces directly.

Placement Locations

Apply small placements inside cabinet hinges, along the back of cabinet shelving, inside outlet and switch plate voids (on the interior face, not inside the electrical box), under the sink, around plumbing penetrations, behind the refrigerator and dishwasher, and along any active foraging trail on baseboards or walls. Use "bait tacos" (small folded cardboard or cut glue board plastic) as a clean placement pallet on finished surfaces.

Important Notes

Do not apply gel bait on or near surfaces treated with Alpine WSG. Residual liquid on the same surface will contaminate the bait and deter feeding. Space bait placements at least 12 inches away from any sprayed surface. Do not apply to food prep surfaces, open food storage areas, or dishes.

Interior Liquid Application

Apply Alpine WSG at the high label rate using a small handheld sprayer for interior surface treatments. Target baseboards, behind appliances, and areas where foraging trails have been observed.

Application Areas

Treat baseboards throughout affected rooms, behind and under the refrigerator and dishwasher, under the kitchen sink cabinet interior, around plumbing penetrations, and along any identified foraging trails on floors or walls. Do not apply to food prep surfaces, open food storage, or dishes.

Mix Rate

Moisture and Conducive Condition Recommendations

Interior Carpenter Ant infestations are almost always connected to a moisture problem. Treatment will suppress activity but long-term control requires addressing the root cause. Communicate the following to the customer:

1. Fix any plumbing leaks, especially under sinks and around dishwasher connections.

2. Address any roof leaks, gutter overflow, or moisture intrusion through the foundation or crawlspace.

3. Improve ventilation in crawlspaces and attics to reduce humidity levels.

4. Replace any water-damaged or decayed wood identified during inspection.

5. Seal gaps around plumbing penetrations and utility entries where ants may be gaining access.

If active Carpenter Ant galleries are found in structural members, advise the customer to consult a licensed contractor for a structural assessment. Document all findings thoroughly in FieldRoutes and notify your manager.

2. Do not clean or wipe down baseboards and treated surfaces for at least 2 weeks to preserve the residual.

Best Practice: Interior and exterior Carpenter Ant protocols should be performed together whenever possible. Interior treatment alone without addressing the exterior source colony will result in reinfestation.

A thorough inspection is the most important step in small fly control. Chemical treatment without identifying and addressing the breeding source will only provide temporary reduction. Inspect the following areas systematically:

Cleaning closets - mop heads, mop buckets, dirty water in floor machine compartments.

Bar mats, rubber floor mats, speed rails, soda gun holsters, ice bin drains, and anywhere liquid can pool and ferment. These are extremely high-risk areas for fruit fly breeding. Inspect the underside of bar equipment and the drains on any ice bins.

Drain Trick

To confirm fly activity in a specific drain, invert a 12-16oz plastic cup lined with a small glue board or sticky tape over the drain opening. Return in a few hours - if flies are present in that drain, they will be caught on the sticky surface. Use this method to prioritize which drains need the most attention.

Exterior Inspection

Check dumpster areas, grease traps, condensate drains, HVAC systems, and any areas with pooling water or organic buildup near the structure. Also check all door and window seals - if daylight is visible around a closed door, flies can enter.

4
Drain Bio-Sanitation Treatment

Drains are the most common small fly breeding site. Physical cleaning must come first - apply product around the drain plate and opening, not just down the drain. Use a drain brush to physically break up and remove biofilm from the drain walls before applying bio-sanitation products.

💧 Nibor-D - Drain Treatment

Mix Nibor-D at 10% solution for initial treatments (two 8 oz. packets or 10 scoops per gallon of water). For severe infestations use a 15% solution. Apply using a foamer if available - foam provides superior 360-degree coverage of the drain walls where larvae are feeding. If foaming equipment is not available, pour the solution carefully around the edges of the drain to coat the biofilm on the drain walls, not just flush straight down. Allow to dwell before flushing. Nibor-D diffuses into the slime coating and contaminates the food source of developing larvae, eliminating them at the source.

Nibor-D (Disodium Octaborate Tetrahydrate) - Nisus
🧪 Bac-Azap - Bio-Sanitation Follow-Up

After Nibor-D treatment, apply Bac-Azap as a follow-up bio-sanitation treatment to drains and other affected surfaces. Bac-Azap uses microorganisms to consume and eliminate the organic matter that small flies breed in, reducing future buildup between services. Apply around the drain opening and plate, to bar mats, rubber mats, mop sinks, and any surface with organic residue. Bac-Azap can also be left with the customer for use between visits as part of their daily cleaning program - it is not a pesticide and does not require a license to apply.

Bac-Azap (Microbial Bio-Sanitation) - Nisus
🦠 DSV - Disinfection and Contact Kill

Apply Nisus DSV as a disinfectant and contact kill treatment to drain areas, surfaces, and any areas with fly activity. DSV is labeled as a contact pesticide for small flies and larvae in drains and other areas, and can be combined with Nibor-D for a faster contact kill. Add 1.1 oz. of DSV to the Nibor-D solution to provide simultaneous disinfection and insecticidal action. Also apply DSV to non-food-contact surfaces and areas around drains, under equipment, and in cleaning closets to disinfect and reduce organic odors that attract flies.

DSV Commercial Disinfectant (Quaternary Ammonium) - Nisus
Do not apply DSV and Bac-Azap together or immediately after one another. DSV is a disinfectant and will kill the microorganisms in Bac-Azap. Apply DSV first, allow surfaces to dry, then apply Bac-Azap separately.
5
Adult Fly Baiting

While drain treatments address larvae and breeding sources, Alpine Fly Bait aerosol provides a fast-acting kill of adult flies already present in the space. Adult fly reduction is important for immediate customer satisfaction and to reduce the number of females actively laying eggs.

🎯 Placement and Application

Apply Alpine Fly Bait aerosol as a spot treatment to non-food-contact surfaces where adult flies are resting or congregating. Target the undersides of shelves, interior walls of cabinets, the backs of equipment, trash can lids, and other surfaces away from food prep areas. Apply small spots or streaks - do not saturate. The bait attracts adult flies which feed on it and are eliminated.

Restrictions

Do not apply to food contact surfaces, food prep areas, or any surface that may come into contact with food. Do not apply where food is exposed or where food is being prepared, served, or processed. In food handling areas, applications must be limited to crack and crevice and out-of-the-way surfaces only. Always read the label before application.

Alpine Fly Bait Aerosol (Dinotefuran, (Z)-9-Tricosene) - BASF
6
Conducive Condition Recommendations

Long-term small fly control is impossible without addressing sanitation. Chemical treatments suppress activity but breeding will resume if the organic sources are not eliminated. Communicate the following recommendations clearly to the customer or facility manager:

1. Deep-clean all floor drains at least weekly using a drain brush and bio-sanitation product - standard drain cleaner poured straight down is not sufficient.

2. Empty, rinse, and clean all trash and recycling bins regularly - inspect inside liners for debris and residue.

3. Clean bar mats, rubber floor mats, and speed rails daily - these are common breeding sites that are frequently overlooked.

4. Address any plumbing leaks, standing water, or drainage issues immediately.

5. Keep mop buckets emptied and mop heads clean and dry between uses.

6. Remove overripe or decaying produce promptly - do not leave fruit or vegetables exposed overnight.

7. Ensure door seals, window screens, and exterior vents are in good repair to prevent entry.

Customer Participation is Essential: Small fly control is a partnership. Even the best chemical program will fail without customer cooperation on sanitation. Leave Bac-Azap with the customer for daily drain maintenance between your service visits.
7
Post-Service Communication and Follow-Up

Before leaving, advise the customer of the following:

1. Do not run water or flush treated drains for at least 2-4 hours after treatment to allow products to dwell and work on the biofilm.

2. They may continue to see adult fly activity for several days as the treatment takes effect - existing adults will die off, but activity will decrease as larvae are eliminated.

3. If they have been provided Bac-Azap, apply to drains daily or as often as possible between service visits.

4. If phorid fly activity was identified and a broken drain line is suspected, they should contact a plumber promptly - no pest control treatment will resolve a sewage source issue.

5. A follow-up visit should be scheduled in 2-3 weeks to assess progress, retreat any problem drains, and evaluate whether any additional sources were missed during the initial inspection.

Document everything in FieldRoutes: Note all drains treated, areas inspected, fly species identified, conducive conditions observed, and all customer recommendations made. Small fly accounts require thorough documentation to track progress and support follow-up visits.

1
Identification and Biology

Correct identification is critical. Carpet beetle damage is frequently misidentified as clothes moth damage. Understanding the life cycle is equally important - all damage is caused by larvae, not adults. Adult beetles do not feed on fabrics.

Adult Beetle

Approximately 1/10 inch long. Black with an irregular pattern of white, brown, and dark yellow scales on wing covers. Older adults may appear solid brown or black as scales wear off. Found near windows in spring and early summer as they attempt to fly outdoors to feed on nectar and pollen.

Larvae (The Damaging Stage)

Slightly longer than adults. Covered in dense, bristly tufts of hair that extend upright when disturbed. Alternating light and dark brown stripes. Broader at the rear, narrower at the front - distinctive from other carpet beetle larvae. Leave behind brown, shell-like cast skins when molting.

Life Stage Duration Notes
Egg 10-20 days to hatch Up to 40 eggs per female; laid on or near food sources
Larva 220-630 days All fabric damage occurs here; feeds in dark, undisturbed locations
Pupa 10-13 days Inactive stage; may be found in same areas as larvae
Adult Female 2-6 weeks; Male 2-4 weeks Feeds outdoors on pollen/nectar; found near windows indoors
Carpet Beetle vs. Clothes Moth: Carpet beetles typically damage a large concentrated area on one portion of a fabric. Moth damage appears as scattered holes throughout. Carpet beetle larvae also leave behind bristly cast skins and do not produce webbing - the presence of cast skins and absence of webbing confirms carpet beetles.
2
Arrival and Customer Interaction

Park in the road or to the side of the home. Clock in to FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask the customer where they have seen damage or live beetles, which materials have been affected (carpets, clothing, rugs, stored items), and how long the problem has been occurring. Ask whether they have recently brought in cut flowers, animal hides, taxidermy, antique rugs, or other natural fiber items. Ask if they have a crawlspace, attic, or areas with bird, wasp, or rodent nests - these are critical harborage sources that are frequently overlooked.

3
Inspection and Source Identification

Finding the source is the most important step. Carpet beetles can disperse widely through a structure and feed in obscure, undisturbed locations. A treatment without a thorough source search will fail. Use a flashlight and inspect methodically.

Interior Priority Inspection Areas

Carpets and rugs: Inspect the edges of all carpeted areas and the carpet beneath heavy furniture - these are the most commonly missed areas. Lift rugs and inspect both the underside of the rug and the floor beneath it.

Closets and storage: Inspect shelving where woolens, furs, and natural fiber clothing are stored. Lint accumulation in corners and along baseboards is a primary larval food source.

Upholstered furniture: Inspect under cushions, along the underside and back of sofas and chairs, and inside furniture in seldom-used or darkened corners. Check any furniture stuffed with natural hair or feathers.

Air ducts: Heat ducts and cold-air return vents accumulate lint, pet hair, and dead insects - an ideal larval food source. Check accessible duct openings.

Taxidermy and animal products: Mounted specimens, animal hides, piano felts and hammers, natural bristle brushes, feather items, silk, leather-bound books, and any stored animal-origin products.

Windows: Adult beetles are commonly found near windows in spring, attempting to exit to feed outdoors. Finding adults at windows is a sign of an active interior infestation or an entry point from outside.

Exterior Inspection

Inspect the exterior of the structure for bird nests, wasp nests, bee nests, and active spider webs - particularly in eaves, soffits, attic vents, and around window frames. These nests contain dead insects, feathers, beeswax, and pollen that serve as outdoor breeding grounds. Remove all accessible nests as part of the service. Carpet beetles frequently fly in from outdoor flowers - assess whether flowering plants and shrubs immediately surrounding the structure may be a source of adult beetles entering the home.

Sticky Trap Monitoring

Place sticky traps near windows during spring to intercept adult beetles entering from outdoors. Place food-baited sticky traps along floor perimeters and in corners to monitor larval activity levels and identify hotspots. Check traps once or twice per week and use findings to guide treatment focus on follow-up visits.

4
Chemical Treatment

Chemical treatment is a supplement to sanitation and exclusion - not a replacement for them. Target treatments to areas where larvae are active. Do not apply insecticides directly to fabrics.

💧 Liquid Spray - Surface Treatments

Apply as spot treatments or along edges of floor coverings, under rugs and furniture, floors and walls of closets, shelving where susceptible fabrics are stored, cracks and crevices, and other lint-accumulating areas. For infested carpet, spray both sides where possible - apply a lighter spray to the upper surface to minimize staining risk. Allow carpet to fully dry before placing furniture or foot traffic on it. If the rug pad contains animal hair or wool, treat it as well.

Product with Bifenthrin, Beta-Cyfluthrin, Lambda-Cyhalothrin, or Permethrin - label must list carpet beetles
🌫️ Insecticidal Dust - Voids and Inaccessible Areas

Use insecticidal dust for attic spaces, wall voids, heat ducts, and other areas where spray is impractical. Apply with a hand duster. Always wear a respirator when treating attics and wall voids with dust - some dust formulations can adversely affect people with respiratory conditions. Apply a light, even coating - over-application reduces effectiveness.

Insecticidal Dust with Deltamethrin or Diatomaceous Earth - label must list carpet beetles
Do not apply insecticides directly to fabrics, clothing, or items the customer intends to continue using. Recommend dry cleaning or laundering for contaminated garments. Chemical treatment of stuffed furniture (hair or feather-stuffed) is not effective by surface spray alone - advise the customer to contact a fumigation specialist for these items.
5
Exclusion

Adult varied carpet beetles fly in from outdoors - exclusion is essential to prevent reinfestation from exterior sources. Address all identified entry points before leaving.

Exterior Nest and Web Removal

Remove all accessible bird, wasp, bee, and rodent nests and old spider webs from the exterior of the structure. These are active breeding grounds and a direct source of carpet beetle pressure on the home. Document their location in FieldRoutes and advise the customer to continue monitoring for new nest construction.

Entry Point Sealing

Ensure all window screens are intact and properly seated. Check door seals and attic vent screens for gaps. Seal any gaps around utility penetrations where beetles may enter. Adult beetles are active fliers - even small gaps in screens are sufficient entry points.

Landscape Recommendation

Advise the customer that eliminating flowering plants and shrubs immediately surrounding the structure will significantly reduce adult beetle pressure from outside. Adult beetles feed on pollen and nectar and are attracted to flowering plants adjacent to the home. Recommending they inspect cut flowers before bringing them indoors is also important - adult beetles frequently hitch rides in on cut flowers.

6
Customer Sanitation and Prevention Recommendations

Carpet beetles are one of the most difficult indoor pests to fully eradicate. Long-term success is impossible without the customer's active participation in sanitation. Educate them clearly on the following:

🧹 Vacuuming and Cleaning

Frequent, thorough vacuuming of carpets, rugs, draperies, upholstered furniture, and closets is one of the most effective control methods. Vacuuming removes food sources, eggs, larvae, and adults simultaneously. The vacuum bag must be disposed of immediately after vacuuming infested areas - it can contain live eggs, larvae, and adults that will escape if left in place.

👕 Fabric Treatment

Badly infested items should be discarded. All washable items should be laundered in hot water or dry cleaned - this kills all life stages. Items being stored long-term (woolens, furs, linens) should be thoroughly inspected, cleaned, and then sealed in protective plastic bags or airtight containers before storage. Non-washable items can be sealed in plastic bags and placed in a freezer for a minimum of two weeks - allow items to return to room temperature inside the sealed bag before removing to prevent condensation damage.

🏠 Lint and Debris Removal

Lint must be removed from cracks, crevices, and areas around baseboards. This includes along the edges of carpets, inside closets, inside air duct openings, and under heavy furniture. Lint, hair, and dead insects are primary larval food sources and their elimination directly reduces the population's ability to sustain itself.

🏺 Proper Storage of Susceptible Items

Valuable furs, woolens, and items of animal origin should be stored in cold storage or airtight garment bags. Before storing, inspect all items carefully to confirm they are pest-free. Naphthalene (mothballs) or paradichlorobenzene placed inside storage containers - not in direct contact with items - will prevent fabric pest damage. Do not use a tight chest or cabinet alone as a long-term solution - these are not airtight.

🎹 Special Cases - Pianos and Taxidermy

Piano felts and hammers can become heavily infested and affect instrument tone and action. Advise the customer to contact a piano technician who may recommend synthetic felt replacements. Mounted animal specimens and game trophies should be cleaned regularly or periodically placed in a freezer for 10-14 days to kill any active infestation.

Fumigation for Stuffed Furniture: Items stuffed with natural hair or feathers (furniture, mattresses, pillows) cannot be effectively controlled by surface spray alone. If the customer has heavily infested stuffed items, advise them to contact a pest control firm with fumigation vault capabilities. Fumigation kills all life stages but does not prevent reinfestation.
7
Post-Service Communication and Follow-Up

Set clear expectations with the customer before leaving. Carpet beetle larvae have an extremely long development period (up to 630 days) which means an infestation can persist for an extended time and multiple service visits are common.

1. Chemical treatment will reduce active larval populations, but larvae feeding in deep carpet pile or inside stored items may not be contacted directly. Sanitation is what eliminates the food source and prevents new generations from developing.

2. Finding adult beetles near windows in spring is normal and does not necessarily indicate a re-infestation - a few adults indoors alone are not cause for alarm. Management is needed when larvae are found actively feeding in fabrics or stored goods.

3. Sticky traps left in place will help monitor activity and confirm whether the population is declining between visits.

4. A follow-up visit should be scheduled to check sticky trap data, assess treatment effectiveness, and retreat any newly identified source areas.

Document all source areas found, items affected, treatment locations, and customer recommendations in FieldRoutes. Carpet beetle accounts require thorough notes because larvae can take months to surface after initial treatment - good documentation allows you to track progress accurately and demonstrate program effectiveness to the customer.

A thorough inspection is the foundation of every rat job. Inspect the full interior including crawlspace and safely accessible attic spaces. Rats can compress their skeletons to squeeze through openings as small as 1/2 inch and can scale vertical surfaces - inspect high as well as low.

Signs of Activity to Look For

Burrows: Typical of Norway rats - found in earthen banks, under concrete slabs, and under walls. Active burrows will be free of dust and cobwebs with compact, hard soil around the main opening. Rub marks may be visible. Roof rat burrows are less common and shallow, near dense vegetation.

Gnaw marks: Fresh gnawings are rough and light-colored. Older gnawings are smoother and often greasy from the rat's coat.

Rub marks: Dark, greasy smears along runways and vertical surfaces. Fresh marks are soft and easily smeared; older marks are dry and flaky.

Droppings: Norway rat droppings are 3/4" (18-20mm) with blunt ends. Roof rat droppings are 1/2" (12-13mm). Fresh droppings are soft and moist; old droppings are dry and hard. Use a blacklight to locate urine stains in frequented areas.

Tracks and runways: Rats consistently follow the same paths along walls. Active runways are free of dust and cobwebs, have greasy rub marks, fresh tracks, and droppings. Exterior runways may run along fences or overhead lines.

Sounds and odors: In established infestations, listen for scratching, gnawing, and squeaking - especially in walls and ceiling spaces at night. A distinct musky odor indicates a well-established population.

Species Identification

Norway Rat: Prefers meat, fish, and cereals. Burrows in the ground. Found in lower areas - under slabs, in crawlspaces, along foundations.

Roof Rat: Prefers fruits, vegetables, and cereals. Excellent climber - found in attics, along rooflines, overhead utility lines, and in upper areas of structures.

2
Trapping Program

Trapping is a core component of every rat job at ClearDefense. Bring 15-30 T-Rex Rat Snap Traps on every rat service. One of the most common technician mistakes is not bringing enough traps or failing to place them in all areas where rats are active.

🪤 T-Rex Rat Snap Traps - Dummy Setting (Initial Visit)

On your first visit, dummy set all traps - place them with bait but do NOT set the trigger. Rats are neophobic (wary of new objects in their environment). Dummy setting gets them comfortable feeding from the traps before the trap is armed. This overcomes bait shyness and dramatically improves catch rates on your follow-up visit.

Place traps frequently along walls and active runways. Do NOT place them in corners - rats tend to slow down at corners and are more likely to avoid traps there. Place traps up high along pipes and in elevated runways. Use zip ties to secure traps to pipes and other elevated surfaces where they would otherwise fall.

Use food the rats have been actively feeding on as trap bait when possible. Provoke attractant may also be used.

🟡 Glue Trays

Glue trays are NOT the same as standard glue boards. Glue trays have a heavy plastic base, are larger, and have a significantly thicker and stickier glue. Adult rats can easily pull themselves free from a standard glue board but have a much more difficult time escaping a glue tray. Use glue trays as a supplement to snap traps, not as a standalone method.

⚔️ The Gauntlet

The Gauntlet is a CDPC trapping system designed to create a lethal passage rats cannot avoid. It consists of two T-Rex snap traps facing in opposite directions with a glue tray sandwiched between them. Place the Gauntlet in a tight run or along a pipe where rats have no option to go around it.

Initial visit: Set up the Gauntlet WITHOUT the glue tray (dummy set only). Follow-up visit: Add the glue tray and arm the snap traps. Zip ties are essential for securing the Gauntlet in elevated positions.

Trap Placement Rule: Place traps along walls and active runways with the trigger end facing the wall. Rats travel along walls and will naturally run directly into the trigger. Never place traps in open areas away from walls or runways.

Exclusion

Every possible entry route must be identified and addressed. Rats can squeeze through openings as small as 1/2 inch. Common entry points include utility line penetrations (water, electric, HVAC, drains, vents), holes in exterior walls, foundation cracks, broken windows, warped doors, and unscreened vents.

Xcluder - Temporary Seal

ClearDefense uses Xcluder poly-resin coated stainless steel fill fabric as a temporary seal for gaps and openings. Standard foam exclusion is not appropriate for rats - rat incisors rate 5.5 on the Mohs Hardness Scale (iron is 5.0), meaning rats can and will chew through foam. Xcluder is the correct temporary material as rats cannot chew through the steel fill.

Long-Term Exclusion - Customer Responsibility

Advise the customer that hardware cloth and mortar are the long-term solution for sealing entry points. This is not a service ClearDefense provides. Make it clear to the customer that permanent exclusion is their responsibility and is essential for long-term control. Document this recommendation in FieldRoutes.

5
Sanitation and Habitat Modification

Sanitation is the backbone of a successful rat control program. Norway rats require 0.8-1.0 oz of food and 15-30ml of water per day. Roof rats require 0.5-1.0 oz of food and 15-30ml of water per day. Both species will travel up to 164 feet from their nest to find food. Eliminating food and shelter sources directly reduces the population's ability to sustain itself.

Exterior Recommendations

Remove rubbish piles, debris, and dense vegetation surrounding the structure. Items should be stored at least 18 inches off the ground and 12 inches away from walls or fences. Garbage bins must have tight-fitting lids. Composting areas should never contain human food scraps. Garden produce should be harvested promptly. Pet food left outdoors must be removed after feeding.

Interior Recommendations

Identify and eliminate potential harborage areas - obscured corners, beneath shelves, cabinets, worktables, and out-of-the-way dark areas. Ensure proper storage so inspections can be conducted thoroughly. Reduce clutter that provides nesting material and shelter.

Habitat Modification for Trapping Success

Actively modify the environment to work in your favor. Remove competing food and water sources so bait and trap attractants are more appealing. Move large items to open up runways and place traps directly in those newly opened runs. The goal is to funnel rat movement through your control devices.

6
Follow-Up Schedule and Adaptation

Rat control is not a one-visit job. Establish a clear follow-up timeline with the customer from the start and communicate it in writing.

Service Timeline

Visit 1 (Initial): Full inspection, dummy set all traps and Gauntlets, load rodent stations (split Fastrac/Contrac), Xcluder exclusion at accessible entry points, full sanitation consultation with customer.

Visit 2 (1.5-2 weeks later): Arm all traps, add glue trays to Gauntlets, check and replenish bait stations, document catches and activity, assess bait preference, adjust placement as needed.

Visits 3+ (every 2 weeks): Continue servicing, adapting trap placement and bait based on activity data, until population is clearly declining.

Maintenance (once control is achieved): Monthly visits until full eradication is confirmed.

Adapt Your Approach

Rat treatments are trial and error. If you are not getting trap hits, move the traps. If consumption on a preferred bait stops, switch to the other. If the current approach is not working, try a completely different one. Document every visit thoroughly in FieldRoutes so you and your team can track progress and adapt intelligently.

Document Everything: Take detailed notes on every visit - what you saw, what was caught, which baits were consumed, and any changes you made. Good notes are what allow us to adapt and succeed on difficult accounts.
7
Common Mistakes to Avoid

Rat control is one of the most challenging services we provide. Avoid these common errors:

Not bringing or setting enough traps. Err heavily on the side of more traps, not fewer. Rats are not mice - they are harder to catch and populations are often larger than they appear.
Poor trap placement. Traps placed in open areas, in corners, or away from active runways will not produce results. Place along walls and in tight runs where rats have no choice but to interact with them.
Underestimating the population. Droppings on the ground are often spread throughout a large area by the rats themselves. Concentrate on active signs - fresh droppings, fresh rub marks, active runways - not just the number of droppings seen.
Not making adjustments when traps are not producing. If you are not getting hits after the second visit, something needs to change - placement, bait, trap type, or all three.
Failing to dummy set traps on the initial visit. Skipping the dummy setting step is one of the most common reasons rat trapping programs fail. Never skip it on a first visit.
Not establishing a proper follow-up schedule. Rat control without a committed follow-up schedule will not succeed. Set the expectation clearly with the customer at the first visit.

2
Identification and Biology

Confirm you are dealing with mice and not young rats before proceeding. Misidentification leads to the wrong control strategy.

Mouse Identification

2.5-3.75" long with a slender body, large ears, and a tail equal in length to the head and body combined. Gray with a lighter underside. Key differentiator from young rats: mice have small feet and a small head. Young rats have noticeably large feet and a large head.

Biology and Habits - Know Your Enemy

Reproduce rapidly: Mature in 35 days with up to 8 litters per year - a small problem becomes a large one quickly. Act with urgency.

Travel range is short: Mice travel only 20-25 feet from their nest. Unlike rats, if you find signs of activity in a specific area, the nest is very close. Concentrate traps in that zone.

Tiny entry points: Mice can fit through a gap the size of a dime (1/4 inch). Exclusion must be extremely thorough.

Neophilic and curious: Unlike rats, mice are naturally curious about new objects in their environment. You do not need to dummy set mouse traps - set them live on the first visit.

3
Inspection

Inspect the full interior with a flashlight before placing any traps. Because mice travel only 20-25 feet from the nest, locating the zone of highest activity is critical to effective trap placement. Inspect the following areas systematically:

Interior Inspection Areas

Kitchen: Inside and behind cabinets, inside drawer spaces, under the stove and refrigerator, inside the toe kick under cabinets, and around all plumbing penetrations under sinks. Look for chewed food packaging, grease marks, and droppings concentrated in specific zones.

Utility areas: Behind water heaters, around HVAC equipment, in laundry rooms, and around any pipe penetrations through walls or floors.

Garage: Along walls, in corners, behind stored items, and around the garage door seal and any penetrations into the living space.

Crawlspace: Along foundation walls, near vents, and at any wood-to-soil contact points. Look for nesting material and active runways.

Attic (if accessible): Near HVAC ducts, insulation disturbance, and any roof penetrations or gaps at the eave line.

Signs of Activity to Document

Droppings: Mouse droppings are 1/8-1/4" long, rod-shaped with pointed ends. Fresh droppings are moist and dark; old droppings are dry, gray, and crumbly. Concentrate of droppings = zone of high activity = where most traps go.

Rub/grease marks: Dark smears along walls and baseboards where mice repeatedly travel. Indicate active runways.

Gnaw marks: On food packaging, wood, plastic pipes, and wiring. Fresh gnawings are light-colored; old gnawings are darker.

Nesting material: Shredded paper, insulation, fabric, or other soft material gathered in a hidden, warm location.

Urine odor: A strong ammonia-like smell in enclosed areas like cabinet interiors or drawers indicates heavy, ongoing activity.

4
Trap Placement - Interior

Interior snap traps are the primary control tool for mice inside the home. Set traps live on the first visit - mice are curious and do not require a dummy-setting period like rats.

🪤 Trapper Mini-Rex Snap Traps - Placement Rules

Place traps perpendicular to the wall with the trigger side facing the wall. Mice run along walls and baseboards - this placement puts the trigger directly in their path. Load with Provoke Mouse Attractant.

Priority placement areas: along active runways and walls, inside cabinet bases and toe kicks, behind the stove and refrigerator, under sinks, inside drawer spaces where droppings are found, and in crawlspaces along foundation walls. Do not place traps in open areas - mice will avoid them.

Do not throw away traps between visits. The scent of previously caught mice on a trap significantly improves future catch rates. Reuse traps unless they are broken.

Exclusion

Exclusion is essential for long-term mouse control. Mice can enter through any gap 1/4 inch or larger - roughly the size of a dime. Every potential entry point identified during inspection must be addressed. Trapping alone without exclusion will result in continued re-infestation.

Xcluder Fill Fabric

Use Xcluder stainless steel mesh to seal gaps around pipe penetrations, utility entries, gaps in the foundation sill plate, gaps around HVAC lines, and any other opening 1/4 inch or larger. Xcluder is the appropriate temporary seal material - standard steel wool compresses over time and rusts. Cut Xcluder with snips and pack firmly into the gap.

Common exclusion points: Pipe penetrations under sinks, gaps around exterior utility entries, gaps at the base of garage doors, gaps at the sill plate in the crawlspace, and any crack or void in the exterior foundation.

7
Customer Communication and Follow-Up

Set clear expectations with the customer before leaving. Mouse control typically requires multiple visits and their cooperation is essential.

Advise the customer of the following:

1. Follow-ups are necessary. Schedule the next visit in 3 days. Continue on 3-day intervals until activity clearly begins to decline, then move to weekly until control is achieved.

2. Do not move or throw away traps. The scent of previously caught mice on a trap significantly improves catch rates. Leave traps exactly where they were placed.

3. Maintain sanitation and reduce clutter. Remove competing food sources - store all dry goods in sealed, hard-sided containers. Eliminate cardboard boxes, paper bags, and fabric piles that mice can use for nesting.

4. Store food securely. Pet food left out overnight is one of the most common reasons mouse programs fail. Feed pets what they will eat and remove the bowl.

5. Permanent exclusion is their responsibility. Reinforce that Xcluder is a temporary measure and that gaps must be permanently sealed with hardware cloth, caulk, or mortar for a long-term solution.

Document Everything in FieldRoutes: Record all areas inspected, trap locations, entry points found and sealed, station placements, and all customer recommendations. Good notes allow you and your team to track progress and adjust the program effectively on follow-up visits.
